# Independent audit: the primary very-well-poised Rodrigues match

Verdict: accepted for the finite raising-operator specialization and its stated
limits. Root read the archived primary text around Definition3.2, Theorem3.4,
Proposition5.1 and the proof of Theorem5.3, and independently checked the scaling
in balanced-rodrigues-primary-source-comparison.md.

The source has R+4 numerator parameters and R denominator parameters. With
R=2m-2, the tuple(0,1,...,1) contains2m+2 exponents, as required. Taking all
b's to0 is regular in the product/operator expression; one must indeed avoid
substitution into separate reciprocal hypergeometric parameters. The source
coefficient is exactly -q^(-1/2)z^(-2) times its numerator product after this
limit, and all a exponents shift by s/2 at the sth step.

Under z=q^x, the Askey-Wilson denominator contributes
(q^(1/2)-q^(-1/2))(q^x-q^(-x))~2x(1-q)². The numerator difference contributes
-2(1-q)^(2m+2)[A_s(x)f(x+1/2)-A_s(-x)f(x-1/2)]. Hence the raising operator
is asymptotic to -2(1-q)^(2m)B_s. Its finite n-fold composition yields exactly
the stated(-2)^n(1-q)^(2mn) factor. Limits on compact sets away from the finite
shifted poles are sufficient for this meromorphic identity; the polynomial
limit extends through its apparent singularities. No integral interchange or
limiting orthogonality theorem is being assumed.

The source's antisymmetric weight also has the stated q-gamma shape.
Gamma(1-2x)=-2x Gamma(-2x) explains the extra-1/(2x) factor. This is useful
context, but the finite-operator proof already establishes the concrete match.
The source's scalar orthogonality does not automatically provide mn balanced
conditions or the arithmetic estimates. The note correctly keeps those separate.
Thus the Rodrigues mechanism has an explicit classical ancestor; this audit
does not establish novelty or lack of novelty for each additional deduction.
