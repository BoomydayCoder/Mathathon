# Independent review of the explicit multiple-orthogonal polynomial

Status: accepted for the precise balanced index (1,...,1). Root independently
checked the complete proof and recomputed 45 rational moment equations for m=1
through 9. Historical priority and growing balanced-index families remain open.

The triangular change of weights is invertible because its diagonal entries
are pi^(2j). For nonincreasing indices, every earlier orthogonality condition
needed for elimination is present. Rational coefficients follow when the
rational moment matrix is nonsingular; this does not establish normality
for all multi-indices.

For the stated polynomial, the two signs in a shifted moment multiply to
(-1)^j. Differentiation of x^(2m+1+2e) exactly gives the factorial ratio in
the Bernoulli moment formula. The identity with Q(x)-Q(x-1), followed by
Bernoulli translation, evaluates Q^(2j)(-1); this vanishes because its root
multiplicity is 2m+1>2j. This proves all equations for every m, not merely
the finite tested cases. The leading coefficient is one and the imaginary-
part expression gives all m distinct positive cotangent-squared roots.

The uniqueness argument is valid: a polynomial of degree at most m-1 has
at most m-1 sign changes on the positive axis. A polynomial of the same
sign-change degree in the strictly decreasing variable csch(pi*y)^2 can
match those changes. The resulting product has fixed nonzero sign, but its
integral would be a linear combination of zero moments. All integrals at
zero remain finite since the worst component behaves as y^0; at infinity
exponential decay dominates. This establishes nonsingularity at (1,...,1).

The polynomial is not the rank-one Schur minimizer and, with m varying, is
not yet an approximation sequence for any fixed odd zeta value. These
scope limitations in the original note are essential and accurate.
