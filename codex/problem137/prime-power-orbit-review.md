# Independent audit of the prime-power tree-orbit theorem

**Verdict.** The proposed argument is valid, with the zero-internal-type cases and the ambient group specified as below. It gives a p-group proof of the exact p-adic valuation for full p^e-ary tree counts. The original enumeration audit below uses multivariable Lagrange inversion; the subsequent `cyclic-type-cycle-splicing.md` replaces that step by an explicit combinatorial bijection. Neither route uses a factorial-valuation formula or Kummer theorem to establish the valuation. No historical-novelty claim is made. The exact official statement has now been retrieved in `codex/sources/problem137-official.md` and explicitly requests this prime-power-arity scope.

## 1. Precise theorem

Let p be prime, e>=1, q=p^e, n>=0, and let

    T_q(n)=binom(qn,n)/((q-1)n+1),
    L=(q-1)n+1=sum_j d_j p^j, 0<=d_j<p,
    s=sum_j d_j, D=(s-1)/(p-1).

Then D is a nonnegative integer and

    v_p(T_q(n))=D,
    T_q(n)/p^D = (-1)^D/product_j d_j!  (mod p).       (1)

The second congruence is interpreted in F_p; every denominator is a unit. The proof exhibits all minimal p-group orbits and counts them modulo p. It is stronger than merely classifying when T_q(n) is a p-unit.

## 2. Expansion and the stable finite group action

Replace each q-ary internal vertex by a perfect p-ary block of height e, matching the q ordered children to length-e words in lexicographic order. The image consists **exactly** of full plane p-ary trees whose leaves all have depth0 modulo e.

To prove the converse, a non-leaf root in such a tree cannot have a leaf at depths1,...,e-1. Its first e levels therefore form the required perfect block. Apply the same argument recursively to its q subtrees at depth e. This reconstructs a unique full q-ary tree and preserves the number of leaves.

A full p-ary tree with L leaves has H=(L-1)/(p-1) internal vertices, so its height is at most H. Use the finite ambient group G_H generated by cyclic rotations of the p children at every vertex of the complete p-ary tree of height H. It is an iterated wreath product of C_p and hence a p-group. Its automorphisms preserve depth, ancestry, arity and leaf count. In particular the expanded q-tree subset is stable. A smaller height en also suffices on that subset, but using H permits direct comparison with all p-ary trees having L leaves.

This is the richer local Sylow action, not cyclic rotation of all q children at once. The latter action can have a smaller minimum orbit exponent whose minimum-orbit count is divisible by p, as the q=4,n=28 example demonstrates.

The cycle-lemma enumeration of full q-ary trees identifies the cardinality of this stable subset with T_q(n). No arithmetic divisibility premise is needed for that enumeration.

## 3. Minimum orbits in the ambient p-tree set

At a non-leaf root, let child orbit exponents be delta_1,...,delta_p. If their orbit types are not all equal, their cyclic tuple has period p and the root exponent is1+sum delta_i. If all types agree, its exponent is p times the common child exponent.

Induction using digit subadditivity proves every orbit exponent is at least D=(s_p(L)-1)/(p-1). In the first case, equality requires each child to be minimal and addition of its leaf counts to be carry-free. In the repeated-child case, equality is possible only when the tree is perfect: the common child exponent and its target lower bound must both be zero.

Consequently a minimal tree has the following unique description. Contract each maximal perfect subtree of height j to a leaf labelled j. Its skeleton is a full p-ary tree with D internal vertices and d_j leaves labelled j. Conversely, every such labelled skeleton expands to a minimal tree.

Here the restriction d_j<p prevents hidden automorphisms. If all p child orbit types at a skeleton vertex coincided, every leaf label occurring in one child would occur at least p times globally, a contradiction. Likewise accidental perfect merging after expansion would require, at a bottommost merged vertex, p equal-height labelled perfect children. This is also impossible. Thus every such skeleton orbit has exactly p^D elements. This is an orbit-size assertion; it does not assert that the entire ambient group has order p^D or acts freely.

Contraction and expansion commute with the ambient rotations. Moreover the expanded tree lies in the q-tree subset exactly when each skeleton leaf at depth i with label j satisfies

    i+j=0 (mod e).                                    (2)

This proves that the required minimum orbits are precisely the orbits of the depth-compatible labelled skeletons. Repeated labels do not create an additional division factor: they are already accounted for in the plane label count, and the orbit size p^D has been proved independently.

## 4. Type counts exist, are unique, and are nonnegative

Types are depths modulo e. Put

    l_i=sum_(j=-i mod e) d_j, 0<=i<e,

and let k_i be the numbers of internal skeleton vertices of each type. Every vertex except the root is a child, so necessarily

    k_i+l_i=delta_(i,0)+p k_(i-1),                    (3)

with indices modulo e. Its unique rational solution is

    (q-1)k_i=sum_(r=0)^(e-1) p^r l_(i-r)-p^i.        (4)

The numerator for i=0 is congruent to L-1 modulo q-1, since p^e=1 modulo q-1. The numerator for general i is congruent to p^i times that numerator. Thus all k_i are integers.

Each numerator in(4) is at least1-p^i, hence strictly greater than -(q-1). Being divisible by q-1, it cannot be negative. Therefore all k_i>=0. Summing(3) gives sum k_i=(s-1)/(p-1)=D.

If D=0 then s=1, L is a power of p, and L=1 modulo p^e-1 forces its exponent to be divisible by e. There is exactly one compatible single labelled leaf and no internal skeleton vertices.

If D>0 then k_0>=1. Indeed k_0=0 forces k_1=l_1=0, and then all subsequent k_i,l_i vanish, leaving l_0=1 and D=0. Furthermore, if any k_i=0 when D>0, propagation in(3) around the cycle shows k_0=1 and l_0=0. This last observation is essential for the zero-index case in the inversion formula below; it is not valid to divide by every k_i unconditionally.

## 5. Exact plane skeleton enumeration

Let U_i count internally rooted plane full p-ary trees of root type i, with z_i marking internal vertices and x_i marking leaves of type i. The recursions are

    U_i=z_i(x_(i+1)+U_(i+1))^p.                      (5)

For D>0, the desired unlabelled-type count is [z^k x^l]U_0. Multivariable Lagrange inversion applies over the rational-function field in the x_i, with formal z_i. Its determinant is

    det(I-u_j partial_(u_j)log Phi_i)
        =1-p^e product_i u_i/(x_i+u_i),

where Phi_i=(x_(i+1)+u_(i+1))^p. For e=1 this is the same one-by-one formula; it is not an excluded case.

The first coefficient contribution is

    B=product_i binom(p k_(i-1), k_i-delta_(i,0))
      =k_0 product_i (p k_i)!/(k_i! l_i!).

When all k_i>0, the second contribution divided by B is

    p^e product_i [(k_i-delta_(i,0))/(p k_(i-1))]
       =(k_0-1)/k_0.

If some k_i=0, section4 gives k_0=1. The second contribution then vanishes: it contains at least u_0^2 while the target u_0 exponent is1. Expansions of the remaining factors as series in u have no negative u powers, even if a factor (x_i+u_i)^-1 occurs. Thus this case has exactly the same result B/k_0, without an invalid division by zero.

The type-only plane skeleton count is therefore

    product_i (p k_i)!/(k_i! l_i!).

At the l_i ordered leaf positions of type i, assign the allowed labels j=-i modulo e with multiplicities d_j. Their multinomial count is l_i!/product_(j=-i mod e)d_j!. Multiplication gives the total number N of compatible plane labelled skeletons:

    N=product_i (p k_i)!/[product_i k_i! product_j d_j!].    (6)

The D=0 single-leaf case agrees with(6), but was proved separately, so the inversion step never divides by k_0=0.

The Lagrange theorem is the only substantial enumeration input added to the prime-arity skeleton proof. Its use is an exact coefficient identity; no asymptotic approximation, genericity of the integer type counts, or analytic convergence assumption is needed.

## 6. Why the number of minimum orbits is a p-unit

Every compatible minimal orbit has p^D elements, so its number is N/p^D. The group action has already proved this quotient is an integer. Since sum k_i=D, formula(6) can be rewritten exactly as

    N/p^D = [product_i product_(1<=a<=p k_i, p does not divide a) a]
                      /product_j d_j!.

This follows by extracting the multiples p,2p,...,p k_i from each factorial; their product is p^(k_i)k_i!. All displayed numerator and denominator factors are p-units. More precisely, splitting the numerator into consecutive blocks of length p and pairing nonzero residues with their inverses gives

    N/p^D = (-1)^D/product_j d_j! !=0 (mod p).

All larger orbits have cardinality divisible by p^(D+1). Divide the total tree count by p^D and reduce modulo p. Only the minimum orbits remain, proving both statements in(1). This completes the proof without using Legendre's factorial-valuation formula to determine the answer.

## 7. Audit boundaries and computational checks

No gap was found in expansion stability, the equality classification, contraction equivariance, label multiplicities, or the typed count once the zero-index cases are separated as above. The proof still uses elementary base-p digit arithmetic to characterize carry-free addition, and uses multivariable Lagrange inversion for enumeration; calling it entirely free of algebraic enumeration would overstate its scope.

The script `check_prime_power_orbit_review.py` independently counts plane p-ary skeletons by depth-residue leaf profiles, compares their labelled counts with(6), and checks the leading-unit congruence against exact Fuss–Catalan integers. Its dynamic program does not use the factorial skeleton formula to generate the skeleton counts. The finite tests supplement the general argument.

This theorem concerns p dividing the arity in the special form q=p^e, which matches the now-retrieved official problem's requested scope. It does not establish a full orbit proof of valuations for arbitrary arity q and arbitrary prime p. The later cycle-splicing bijection removes the algebraic enumeration dependency recorded in this original audit; see that note for the resulting fully combinatorial route and its additional checks.
