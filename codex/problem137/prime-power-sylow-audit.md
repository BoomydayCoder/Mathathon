# Independent audit: exact valuations for all prime-power arities via Sylow actions

Status: complete audit of the parent agent's stronger proof construction, extending tree-pgroup-proof.md from q=p to q=p^e. The skeleton enumeration uses the multivariable Lagrange–Good coefficient formula, explicitly stated and specialized below. That general theorem is the only unproved machinery here; it can instead be replaced by a suitable multitype cycle lemma. No historical novelty claim. A historical comparison with existing p-group proofs is still required.

Let p be prime, e>=1, q=p^e, n>=0,

L=(q-1)n+1=sum_j d_j p^j, 0<=d_j<p,
s=sum_j d_j,
D=(s-1)/(p-1).

**Theorem.** The full q-ary tree count T_q(n)=binom(qn,n)/L satisfies

v_p(T_q(n))=D.

More precisely a natural finite p-group acting on these trees has all orbit sizes divisible by p^D, and its minimum-orbit count M satisfies

M congruent (-1)^D / product_j d_j! (mod p),

hence is nonzero. In fact the leading p-adic unit is determined as well:

p^{-D} T_q(n) congruent (-1)^D / product_j d_j! (mod p).

The formula for the count M itself is given below.

## 1. Stable expansion and the correct group

Replace each internal q-ary vertex by a perfect p-ary tree of height e, attaching its q ordered children at the terminal slots in lexicographic order. This embeds full q-ary trees into full p-ary trees. Its image consists exactly of p-ary trees all of whose leaves have depth divisible by e. Indeed within an e-level block starting at a vertex of depth divisible by e, a leaf cannot occur at an intermediate depth, so the block is either a leaf immediately or a complete perfect block of height e. Collapse those blocks recursively to obtain the inverse.

A q-ary tree with n internal vertices has height at most n. Its expansion therefore embeds in the complete p-ary ambient tree of height en. Act by the finite iterated wreath p-group generated by cyclic p-child rotations at every ambient vertex. Ambient automorphisms preserve depth, so the leaf-depth condition is preserved. The expanded q-tree set is therefore a union of full p-group orbits, not merely a subset of their vertices.

The all-p-ary-tree argument in tree-pgroup-proof.md shows that every orbit with L leaves has exponent at least D. Equality orbits are represented uniquely by full p-ary skeletons in which leaf label j appears d_j times and means expansion to a perfect p-ary subtree of height j. Every such skeleton has D internal vertices, and its orbit size is p^D: if a skeleton vertex had p equal child orbit types, each label in those children would be repeated at least p times, contradicting d_j<p. Expansion and maximal-perfect-subtree contraction commute with the group action. A nontrivial accidental perfect merger would, at its deepest skeleton vertex, require p identically labeled child blocks, the same contradiction.

An expanded labeled skeleton has all leaves at depths divisible by e precisely when a skeleton leaf at depth i has label j satisfying i+j=0 modulo e. Thus it remains to count these depth-constrained labeled skeletons. This is the exact additional restriction needed beyond the prime-arity proof.

## 2. Forced numbers of vertices at each depth residue

Let

l_i=sum_{j congruent -i mod e} d_j,

and let k_i be the skeleton's number of internal vertices at depths congruent to i, with 0<=i<e. Slot counting at each residue gives

k_i+l_i=delta_{i0}+p k_{i-1},

where subscripts are read modulo e. Summing gives sum_i k_i=D. Solving gives

k_i=[sum_{r=0}^{e-1} p^r l_{i-r} - p^i]/(q-1).

These numbers are uniquely determined by the leaf digits. They are integers: modulo q-1, p^e=1 and l_h collects digits with j congruent -h, so the weighted sum is congruent to p^i L, hence to p^i since L=1 modulo q-1. They are nonnegative. The weighted sum is positive, while 0<p^i<=p^{e-1}<=q-1; hence the numerator is strictly greater than -(q-1). An integer multiple of q-1 in that interval cannot be negative. This covers q=2,e=1 as well (then p^i=q-1=1).

If D=0, the digits have sum one. The weighted condition forces the sole label j to be divisible by e (equivalently L=q^h). The skeleton is the single labeled leaf, all k_i=0, and M=1.

If D>0, k_0>=1: k_0=0 would make its weighted numerator zero, so the weighted leaf sum is 1; this requires exactly one leaf, with residue label zero, giving D=0. If any k_i vanishes, the constraints propagate zeros forward until the index just before root residue 0. In that case k_0+l_0=1 and k_0>=1, forcing k_0=1. This fact resolves zero-index cases in the coefficient calculation below.

## 3. Typed skeleton count via an explicit Lagrange–Good calculation

First forget the individual labels, retaining only leaf depth residues. Introduce x_i for a leaf of residue i and z_i for an internal vertex of residue i. For skeletons whose root is internal, let U_i satisfy

U_i=z_i (x_{i+1}+U_{i+1})^p.

The coefficient [z^k x^l]U_0 counts plane typed skeletons. We use the standard formal multivariable Lagrange–Good identity in this precise form: if U_i=z_i f_i(U), then

[z^k] H(U)
=[u^k] H(u) product_i f_i(u)^{k_i}
   det(delta_ij - u_j/f_i(u) * partial f_i/partial u_j).

One may work first over the rational-function field in the x_i, where the constant terms f_i(0)=x_{i+1}^p are invertible, and then take the resulting polynomial coefficient in x. Here H(u)=u_0 and f_i=(x_{i+1}+u_{i+1})^p. The determinant is

1-p^e product_i u_i/(x_i+u_i).

This includes e=1, where the determinant is the single diagonal term 1-pu_0/(x_0+u_0).

The first determinant term gives

C_1=product_i binom(p k_{i-1}, k_i-delta_{i0}).

The leaf exponents automatically match l_i by the slot constraints. If all k_i>0, the second determinant term divided by C_1 is

p^e product_i (k_i-delta_{i0})/(p k_{i-1})=(k_0-1)/k_0.

Therefore the difference is C_1/k_0. If some k_i=0, the second term vanishes (it demands at least one u_i where its required exponent is zero), and the preceding section gives k_0=1, yielding the same expression. Thus in all nontrivial cases the unlabelled typed count is

C=product_i (p k_i)! / [product_i k_i! product_i l_i!].

To restore labels, independently distribute label j among the l_i leaf positions in residue i. This multiplies by product_i l_i! / product_j d_j!. Hence the number of plane labeled skeletons is

N_e=product_i (p k_i)! / [product_i k_i! product_j d_j!].

For D=0 the same factorial expression is 1 and agrees with the root-leaf case, which was handled separately rather than included incorrectly in U_0.

## 4. Divide by orbit sizes and reduce modulo p

Every labeled skeleton orbit has p^D elements by Section 1, so M=N_e/p^D. Since sum_i k_i=D,

M= [product_i ((p k_i)!/(p^{k_i} k_i!))] / product_j d_j!.

Each bracket is exactly the product of integers from 1 through p k_i not divisible by p. In k_i complete blocks it is congruent ((p-1)!)^{k_i}=(-1)^{k_i} modulo p, using Wilson's inverse-pairing argument. Every d_j! is nonzero modulo p because d_j<p. Therefore

M congruent (-1)^D / product_j d_j! !=0 modulo p.

All remaining full-tree orbits are divisible by p^{D+1}. Divide their total by p^D: the residue is M, proving the exact valuation D and the stated leading-unit congruence. This argument uses no Kummer or factorial-valuation formula; the only factorial reduction is the explicit product of nonmultiples of p.

## 5. Examples and audit warnings

For q=4,p=2,e=2,n=28, L=85 has four binary 1-digits at j=0,2,4,6. Thus l=(4,0), k=(1,2), D=3, N_e=2!*4!/(1!*2!)=24, and M=24/8=3, odd. The weaker cyclic-four-child action instead has six minimum orbits of size four, illustrating why its minimum exponent does not suffice.

For q=4,n=3, L=10 has digits at j=1,3. Then l=(0,2), k=(1,0), D=1, N_e=2, M=1. This checks the zero-k case: using a formula that divides indiscriminately by every k_i would be invalid, whereas the coefficient argument above treats it correctly.

For q=9,p=3,e=2,n=2, L=17=(122)_3, l=(3,2), k=(1,1), D=2, N_e=(3!)^2/(2!2!)=9, and M=1.
