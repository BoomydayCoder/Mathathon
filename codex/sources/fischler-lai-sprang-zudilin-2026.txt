    The contradiction comes from combining three arguments:

   • An upper bound on the denominator of ∆n,m , obtained in §3 by considering a basis consisting of
     Pochhammer symbols and using standard arithmetic properties. No sophisticated p-adic estimates
     are in use.

   • An upper bound on the absolute value of ∆n,m , obtained in §4 also using Pochhammer symbols and
     elementary estimations.

   • A non-vanishing result that asserts that ∆n,m is nonzero for at least one value of m in a given range;
     this is the subject of §5.

These results are finally combined in §6 to complete the proof.
   The principal novelties here lie in the non-vanishing part, and our exposition in §5 is executed generally
enough to serve for future use in related contexts. It covers Hankel matrices associated with signed
measures on discrete closed subsets of the reals. At the same time our design of the linear functional Ln
deviates significantly from its prototype from [7]. While using Hankel determinants in [7] gives one access
to better arithmetic than by traditional techniques, the improvement in Theorem 1.1 over the previous
bound (1.3) comes entirely from the analytic estimates of the determinants.

then apply it to the determinants ∆n,m .
