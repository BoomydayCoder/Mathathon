# A Lean-checked ζ(5) claim awaits referees

As of 15 November 2026 the irrationality of ζ(5) is **an unrefereed claim under evaluation, not a published theorem**: it rests on one single-version Zenodo preprint by Aabir Fauzan (Aalto University) dated 17 September 2026 ([Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/22826419)). What the requester heard is therefore half right. A proof was announced, three independent Lean 4 projects report that they machine-checked the statement (two with only Lean's standard axioms, one assuming the prime number theorem), and the specialists Lai, Fischler, Sprang and Zudilin already call it "a proof" and reuse its method ([arXiv:2610.06838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06838); [arXiv:2611.16156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16156)). But no journal, arXiv version or human referee report was found, the printed text has gaps that the formalizers had to repair, the quantitative corollary (irrationality exponent at most 260) is unverified, the extent of AI authorship is disputed, and nobody in this search rebuilt the Lean code. The argument is not of Apéry type: it replaces small linear forms in 1 and ζ(5) by Hankel determinants of growing size and wins on the e^(−cn²) scale by about 1.3%. The refereed record still stops short of ζ(5): Apéry's ζ(3), Zudilin's "one of ζ(5), ζ(7), ζ(9), ζ(11)", Lai–Zhou's "two of ζ(5), …, ζ(35)" and asymptotic counts, none of which isolates a single value. Of the three leads, Dan Romik (the correct spelling) directed an AI-written Lean formalization and audit of Fauzan's preprint and wrote no proof of his own; Frank Calegari's theorems concern other constants, and his blog traces the new method to Prévost's Padé approximants; Epoch AI's FrontierMath lists one open problem on the topic, now labelled "Solved (human + AI)", for which no recurrence certificate and no AI run are reported.

## One Zenodo preprint carries the whole ζ(5) claim

### The claim is a single unrevised version

The preprint is "ζ(5) is irrational" by Aabir Fauzan (ORCID 0009-0001-1673-9710, Aalto University), **32 pages, dated 17 September 2026, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22826419, version v1 only** ([Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/22826419); [PDF](https://zenodo.org/records/22826419/files/ZETA5_IS_IRRATIONAL.pdf)). The record's own notes say "This version records the initial presentation of the main construction and argument, while the full manuscript is in preparation" and that "Subsequent revisions will be maintained on arXiv once the paper is announced there" ([Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/22826419)). Four separate arXiv searches on 15 November 2026 found no version by the author ([arXiv search](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Fauzan+zeta%285%29+irrational&searchtype=all&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=50); [arXiv API query](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=au:Fauzan+AND+all:zeta&sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending&max_results=10)). The paper states Theorem 1.1, "The number ζ(5) is irrational", and Corollary 1.2, a bound |ζ(5) − a/b| > b^(−260) for all b beyond an unspecified threshold ([PDF](https://zenodo.org/records/22826419/files/ZETA5_IS_IRRATIONAL.pdf)). The result is stated unconditionally. The prime number theorem enters Proposition 5.2 as a cited classical theorem, so "assuming the prime number theorem" describes one Lean formalization, not the preprint ([PDF](https://zenodo.org/records/22826419/files/ZETA5_IS_IRRATIONAL.pdf); [Romik README](https://github.com/danromik/zeta5-irrationality/blob/fc2fefa77076e22e7e80b4c101ded5a98d53e14d/README.md)).

The method matters for classifying the claim. Fauzan writes that "The proof uses integer polynomials of growing degree, rather than linear forms in 1 and ζ(5)" ([PDF](https://zenodo.org/records/22826419/files/ZETA5_IS_IRRATIONAL.pdf)). With K = 40n, N = 3n and h = 37n, an h×h Hankel matrix is built from a functional whose values are affine in X; at X = ζ(5) the functional is integration against a positive weight, so the determinant is a positive Gram determinant. A logarithmic-energy estimate bounds its size, and a p-adic analysis of the whole determinant, summed with the prime number theorem, clears denominators. The engine is Theorem 2.1: integer polynomials Q_n of degree 37n with **0 < Q_n(ζ(5)) < exp(−139n²/5)**, so that b^(37n)·Q_n(a/b) would be a positive integer tending to zero if ζ(5) = a/b ([PDF](https://zenodo.org/records/22826419/files/ZETA5_IS_IRRATIONAL.pdf)). The final comparison is thin. The real-side constant is U = −2733991/2000000 ≈ −1.3670 per K² and the arithmetic cost is A_200 ≈ 1.3496, a net of about −0.0174, which an AI audit in Romik's repository gives as "1.2899% of A_200" ([PDF](https://zenodo.org/records/22826419/files/ZETA5_IS_IRRATIONAL.pdf); [audit](https://github.com/danromik/zeta5-irrationality/blob/fc2fefa77076e22e7e80b4c101ded5a98d53e14d/docs/zeta5-audit.tex)).

### Frank Calegari proved other constants irrational and traced the new method

Calegari's own theorems concern other numbers. His 2005 paper proves the 2-adic and 3-adic ζ_p(3) and the 2-adic Catalan constant irrational ([arXiv:math/0408214](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0408214)). With Dimitrov and Tang he proved that 1, ζ(2) and L(2, χ₋₃) are linearly independent, by a new "arithmetic holonomy bound" applied to known Apéry-type sequences of Zagier ([arXiv:2408.15403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15403)). Their 2025 ICM paper adds |L(2, χ₋₃) − p/q| > 1/q^24781 and the irrationality of the 2-adic ζ₂(5), and states: "Neither of these results, however, have any direct bearing on the (ir)rationality of the classical ζ(5)" ([arXiv:2510.04156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04156)). The same paper gives the fullest explanation of the difficulty: an Apéry-type scheme needs a motivic local system in which the target appears as a period on its own, periods get "mixed", and "we know of no simple analogous construction for ζ(2k+1) ∈ R", whereas 2-adically ζ_p(2) = 0 separates the odd values ([arXiv:2510.04156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04156)). The linear-independence paper is a 218-page preprint marked "the submitted version"; its community acceptance is signalled by an Oberwolfach Arbeitsgemeinschaft in April 2026, but journal publication was not confirmed ([Persiflage, 4 Jun 2025](https://galoisrepresentations.org/2025/06/04/arbeitsgemeinschaft-2026/)).

His commentary on the 2026 claim is the blog post "zeta(5) is irrational" of 24 September 2026. It accepts correctness on the strength of the Lean verification, voices the AI-authorship impression quoted above, and then traces the ideas: the transform identity behind the positive measure "is the integer-weight specialization of Prévost's Stieltjes representation", and "'All' that has been done is to adjust Prévost's positive measure by D_N^r/D_K for suitable parameters N and K" ([Persiflage](https://galoisrepresentations.org/2026/09/24/zeta5-is-irrational/)). The post states, without the estimates, a uniform theorem for k ∈ {2, 3, 4, 5} with decay constants (30, 96, 6, 3/2), and says part of its literature map was produced by ChatGPT and not fully vetted ([Persiflage](https://galoisrepresentations.org/2026/09/24/zeta5-is-irrational/)). Twelve days before the preprint he had written that "the first person who 'presses a button' and gets a proof seems more or less irrelevant if they added no intellectual content of their own" ([Persiflage, 5 Sep 2026](https://galoisrepresentations.org/2026/09/05/look-mom-i-pressed-a-button/)). By 8 October he still called the new ideas unmotivated and deferred judgment on "whether they are simply new tricks or evidence of something more substantial" to an SLMath conference on irrationality "in January", adding "Apéry's result from almost 50 years ago is still something we don't really understand" ([Persiflage, 8 Oct 2026](https://galoisrepresentations.org/2026/10/08/a-brief-digest-of-some-new-openai-results/)).

The "human + AI" label was introduced in a changelog entry dated 16 September 2026, one day before the preprint, for cases where "AI was instrumental in solving the problem, but did not solve it autonomously", and Epoch counts such cases as AI solutions in its headline tally "Breakthrough 1/3" ([Open Problems hub](https://epoch.ai/frontiermath/open-problems)). The label goes beyond the preprint's own disclosure and Epoch gives no evidence for it. A later update says OpenAI's 6 October 2026 release includes a preprint proving Catalan's constant irrational, "solving another target of this problem"; that proof is also a determinant argument, and OpenAI describes its release as "raw research data" in which "Some of the unformalized results could have issues" ([problem page](https://epoch.ai/frontiermath/open-problems/apery-irrationality); [openai/math](https://github.com/openai/math)). ## The bibliography: one Apéry construction, many derivations, one determinant method

The literature has a simple shape once it is sorted. Apéry's theorem has, in Fischler's words, one construction: "on construit toujours les mêmes formes linéaires", reached by about ten derivations, plus one genuinely larger family due to Rhin and Viola ([Fischler, arXiv:math/0303066](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0303066)). The hypergeometric derivations are the ones that generalise to several odd zeta values, and that generalisation yields collection theorems, never ζ(5) alone. The direct attacks on ζ(5) all end with the same deficit between decay and denominators. The 2026 method abandons single linear forms for determinants.

Nothing is included that is not in the notes.

### Apéry's original approach and the first complete write-ups

Every proof of Apéry's theorem builds rationals u_n, v_n with u_n ∈ ℤ, 2d_n³·v_n ∈ ℤ and |u_n·ζ(3) − v_n|^(1/n) → (√2−1)⁴, and concludes because (√2−1)⁴·e³ < 1; the recurrence is (n+1)³y_(n+1) − (34n³+51n²+27n+5)y_n + n³y_(n−1) = 0 ([Fischler §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0303066)). The margin is 4·log(√2+1) ≈ 3.5255 against 3 ([Calegari–Dimitrov–Tang §1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15403)). None of the primary texts in this group could be opened; their contents are as reported by Fischler, Rajkumar, Cohen and Zeilberger.

### Direct attacks on ζ(5): integrals, recurrences, cellular integrals and searches

Until 17 September 2026 every direct construction fell short, and the sources say so in their own words. Brown and Zudilin's January 2026 revision still reads: "the traditional strategy for approaching the irrationality of ζ(5) is to try to construct linear forms in 1 and ζ(5) with rational coefficients and good arithmetic properties. At the time of writing, it has not succeeded despite many years of effort" ([arXiv:2210.03391](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03391)). The multiple-integral line and the hypergeometric line are reformulations of the same linear forms through Zudilin's identity; the cellular-integral line is a genuinely different framework whose symmetric case reproduces Zudilin's 2002 recursion.

## Linear forms lost to denominators; determinants won by 1.3%

### One ledger explains fifty years of near misses

Every method in the bibliography is a comparison between how fast an approximating quantity decays and how much it costs to clear its denominators. The table collects the documented exponents. Several net figures are the notes' own arithmetic from the published constants and are marked as such.

The ledger shows why ζ(5) resisted. A weight-5 period carries denominators d_n⁵, so a form in 1 and ζ(5) with integer leading coefficient must decay faster than e^(−5n); the forms available after removing ζ(3) or ζ(2) decay like e^(−1.09n) or, in Brown–Zudilin's symmetric case, e^(−2.47n) ([arXiv:math/0206178](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0206178); [arXiv:2210.03391](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03391)). The sources document four separate reasons that proofs of Apéry's theorem did not transfer. **Parasitic terms:** generic weight-5 integrals involve ζ(3), ζ(2)ζ(3) and ζ(3,2), well-poised symmetry removes only even zeta values, and the only route to a form in 1 and ζ(5) passes to "the subleading asymptotic" ([Brown](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.6508); [arXiv:math/0609743](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0609743); [arXiv:2210.03391](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03391)). **Denominators against decay:** accelerated series for ζ(5) exist, but "la croissance des dénominateurs est trop rapide par rapport à la convergence" ([Fischler §1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0303066)). **Individual structure:** the modular proof sees ζ(3) as a special L-value, and Calegari–Dimitrov–Tang report no motivic local system in which a real ζ(2k+1) appears alone ([arXiv:2510.04156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04156)). **Rarity:** WZ-generated schemes are "usually of higher order", Rajkumar's 2×2 construction has no solutions in degree above 3, automated searches found many new irrational constants but not ζ(5), and Lai, Sprang and Zudilin "do not expect" a three-term Apéry-like recursion for the real ζ(5) ([Zeilberger](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~zeilberg/mamarim/mamarimhtml/pun.html); [arXiv:1212.5881](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.5881); [arXiv:2505.05005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05005)). No source proves a no-go theorem; these are documented failures and expectations.

The same machinery, moved to other settings, gave the same shape of result: one of four q-zeta values, one of five beta values, one p-adic value in a window ([arXiv:0712.1762](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.1762); [arXiv:2103.00904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00904); [arXiv:2304.00816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00816)). Where a single value was reached before 2026, the proof used extra structure: modularity for ζ₂(3) and ζ₃(3), holonomy bounds for L(2, χ₋₃) and ζ₂(5) ([arXiv:math/0408214](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0408214); [arXiv:2408.15403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15403)). That pattern supports reading the 2026 claim as a new arithmetic input and not a better choice of hypergeometric parameters.

On the evidence of Fauzan's text and of Lai's and Fischler–Lai–Sprang–Zudilin's descriptions, the determinant method changes three things; this three-part summary is the notes' synthesis, not a quotation. The comparison moves to the n² scale for a determinant of size about n, where the p-adic valuation of the whole determinant is, in Lai's words, "much larger than the entrywise lower bound" ([arXiv:2610.06838](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.06838v2)). Non-vanishing comes from positivity of a moment matrix. The entries are forms in 1 and ζ(5) only, built from Hurwitz-type pole evaluations j⁴(ζ(5) − H_j^(5)), so the ζ(3)-elimination problem does not arise ([PDF](https://zenodo.org/records/22826419/files/ZETA5_IS_IRRATIONAL.pdf)). The lineage is equally specific: the positive measure is Prévost's, the determinant criterion is Zudilin's, and the new step is the modifier D_N⁶/D_K with a proof that coefficient denominators cancel beyond the entrywise bound ([Persiflage](https://galoisrepresentations.org/2026/09/24/zeta5-is-irrational/)). The method sidesteps the "missing differential equation" diagnosis of Calegari, Dimitrov and Tang without contradicting it, and no retrieved source relates it to holonomy bounds, to Zudilin's transfinite-diameter obstruction, or explains why the family stops at k = 5.

### A compact synthesis for a research prompt

> **Status (15 November 2026).** ζ(3) is irrational (Apéry 1978; all known proofs build the same linear forms u_n·ζ(3) − v_n, with u_n ∈ ℤ, 2d_n³·v_n ∈ ℤ and decay (√2−1)^(4n), and conclude from 4·log(√2+1) > 3). Refereed results on higher odd values are collection theorems only: infinitely many ζ(2k+1) are irrational (Ball–Rivoal 2001), with dim_ℚ Span(1, ζ(3), …, ζ(s)) ≥ 0.21·√(s/log s) for large s (Fischler 2026); at least 1.28·√(s/log s) of ζ(3), …, ζ(s) are irrational (Lai 2026), raised to 2.78·√(s·log log s/log s) in an unrefereed preprint (arXiv:2611.16156); one of ζ(5), ζ(7), ζ(9), ζ(11) is irrational (Zudilin 2001); two of ζ(5), …, ζ(35) are irrational (Lai–Zhou 2022). None isolates ζ(5).
>
> **ζ(5) itself.** A. Fauzan's preprint "ζ(5) is irrational" (Zenodo, 17 September 2026, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22826419, v1 only) claims ζ(5) ∉ ℚ. Treat it as an unrefereed claim under evaluation: no peer review found; Theorem 1.1 is reported machine-checked in Lean 4 by three independent projects (mo271/Zeta5 and domino14/zeta5 with standard axioms and a formalized prime number theorem; danromik/zeta5-irrationality with the prime number theorem as an axiom), none rebuilt by the compilers of this summary; Lai and Fischler–Lai–Sprang–Zudilin cite it as a proof (arXiv:2610.06838, arXiv:2611.16156). The printed text has gaps repaired by the formalizers (an unproved inequality on p. 10; a missing term on p. 12; typos). The corollary "irrationality exponent ≤ 260" (≤ 171 in the conditional arXiv:2611.01708) is not formally verified. The extent of AI authorship is disputed.
>
> **Method.** Not Apéry-type. Integer polynomials Q_n of degree 37n with 0 < Q_n(ζ(5)) < exp(−139n²/5), obtained as normalised Hankel determinants det[μ_X(D_N(t)⁶·t^(i+j)/D_K(t))], D_m(t) = ∏(t + j²), K = 40n, N = 3n, where μ_X(1/(t+j²)) = j⁴(X − H_j^(5)) − 1/4 + 1/(2j). Positivity at X = ζ(5) (a Gram matrix for an explicit positive weight) gives non-vanishing; a logarithmic-energy estimate gives size; determinant-level p-adic valuations, summed by the prime number theorem, beat entrywise bounds. Net margin about 1.3% on the K² scale. Antecedents: Prévost's Padé measure, Zudilin's determinantal criterion (arXiv:1507.05697), Brown (arXiv:2604.20741). Calegari reports the family covers ζ(k) for k = 2, 3, 4, 5 "but not (at least directly) for larger k".
>
> **Why earlier attacks failed.** Linear forms in 1 and ζ(5) need decay beyond e^(−5n) against d_n⁵; the best had e^(−1.09n) (Zudilin, arXiv:math/0206178) and worthiness 0.86 < 1 (Brown–Zudilin, arXiv:2210.03391). Obstructions: parasitic ζ(3), ζ(2)ζ(3), ζ(3,2); one power of d_n too many in well-poised series; no modular or second-order (Apéry-limit) structure for the real ζ(5).
>
> **Open as of this date.** Irrationality of ζ(7) and higher odd values individually; any linear independence involving ζ(5); an effective threshold and an independent check for the exponent bound; the relation of the determinant method to holonomy bounds (arXiv:2408.15403) and to transfinite-diameter obstructions; a refereed or revised text of the ζ(5) proof.

## Conclusion

The order of verification has inverted for this problem. A machine check arrived within a week, expert reuse within three, and the human-readable proof is still a first draft with known gaps and an author who has not revised it. For a subsequent research prompt this means three statements should be kept apart and cited differently: that ζ(5) is irrational (cite the Lean statements and their axioms, flagged as reported and not rebuilt), that Fauzan's printed argument is correct as written (it is not complete, on the formalizers' evidence), and who or what found the proof (unknown). The tracker's own test for settlement is the right one: "An expert in the field from outside the team saying the written proof holds, or a journal accepting it" ([tracker](https://ademb10.com/claims/zeta-5)). The first announced venue for such an assessment is the SLMath conference on irrationality that Calegari expects in January ([Persiflage](https://galoisrepresentations.org/2026/10/08/a-brief-digest-of-some-new-openai-results/)).

The mathematics also points somewhere specific. The ledger margins are thin (0.3% for Zudilin's four numbers, 1.3% for Fauzan, 2.5% for Lai), and Calegari's decay constants for the uniform family fall from 96 at k = 3 to 3/2 at k = 5, so ζ(5) sits at the edge of what this determinant family reaches and ζ(7) is not a parameter change away. The productive questions are the ones the specialists themselves raise: where the determinant-level p-adic gain comes from structurally, how it relates to holonomy bounds and to Zudilin's transfinite-diameter obstruction, and whether the method yields linear independence involving ζ(5). Fischler, Lai, Sprang and Zudilin put the priority plainly: "Understanding the beautiful structures behind those methods and proofs seems to us a more exciting task than targeting particular numbers that still await their irrationality status" ([arXiv:2611.16156](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16156)).
