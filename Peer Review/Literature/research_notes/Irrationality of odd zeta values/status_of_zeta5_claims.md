## 1. Who is the author of the 2026 preprint, what is its exact title, where is it posted, what versions exist, and what precisely does it claim?

### Takeaway
The preprint is "ζ(5) is irrational" by Aabir Fauzan (Aalto University), dated 17 September 2026, posted only on Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22826419, a single version v1, 32 pages); no arXiv version by the author was found on 15 November 2026. It claims unconditionally that ζ(5) ∉ ℚ (Theorem 1.1) and, separately, an irrationality-exponent bound ≤ 260 (Corollary 1.2); the prime number theorem is *used* as a classical input, not assumed as a hypothesis.

## 2. What is the method, and how does it relate to earlier work (Apéry, Ball–Rivoal, Zudilin, Brown, Calegari–Dimitrov–Tang)?

### Takeaway
The proof does not use linear forms in 1 and ζ(5) with an Apéry-type recurrence; it uses *integer polynomials of growing degree* obtained as rationally normalised h×h Hankel determinants whose entries are affine in X, shown positive at X = ζ(5) by a positive moment (Gram) representation, small on the e^{−cK²} scale by a logarithmic-energy estimate, and integral after a p-adic analysis of the *whole determinant* summed via PNT. Specialists (Lai; Fischler–Lai–Sprang–Zudilin) place it in the line of Hankel-determinant ideas of Haynes–Zudilin, Zudilin (2017) and Brown, and describe its key gain as "finer p-adic estimates".

### Cited Findings
- **Author's description of the strategy [R].** "The proof uses integer polynomials of growing degree, rather than linear forms in 1 and ζ(5). They are obtained from Hankel determinants whose entries are affine in the polynomial variable. At ζ(5), a positive moment representation makes the determinants nonzero." — [Zenodo PDF](https://zenodo.org/records/22826419/files/ZETA5_IS_IRRATIONAL.pdf).
- **Three ingredients as described in §1.1 [R].** (a) Positivity: "At X = ζ(5), the functional becomes integration against a positive weight, and G_K(ζ(5)) is a positive moment matrix. Its determinant is consequently positive." (b) Real size: "A multiple-integral representation expresses that determinant in terms of a squared Vandermonde factor and the weight. After scaling, the logarithm of the integrand is estimated by a logarithmic-energy inequality. This gives the required real bound on the K² scale." (Andréief's identity is cited from Forrester.) (c) Arithmetic: "For the intermediate primes, a basis with prescribed vanishing at the pole classes gives a determinant valuation. For the larger primes, another basis isolates the possible negative valuations of the residue terms. The remaining polynomial contribution is handled by a rank bound." — [Zenodo PDF](https://zenodo.org/records/22826419/files/ZETA5_IS_IRRATIONAL.pdf).
- **Size of the margin [R].** Final comparison (7.1)–(7.2): limsup K⁻² log Q_{K,M}(ζ(5)) ≤ A_M + U with U = −2733991/2000000 and "−1600(A_200 + U) > 139/5" — [Zenodo PDF](https://zenodo.org/records/22826419/files/ZETA5_IS_IRRATIONAL.pdf).
- **How the preprint positions itself [R].** It cites Zudilin's "at least one of ζ(5), ζ(7), ζ(9), ζ(11) is irrational" and Brown–Zudilin's approximations 0 < |ζ(5) − p/q| < q^−0.86, noting "The exponent 0.86 does not by itself imply irrationality", and quotes Lai–Lupu–Sprang (2025) describing the irrationality of ζ(5) as a problem that "seems beyond reach" — [Zenodo PDF](https://zenodo.org/records/22826419/files/ZETA5_IS_IRRATIONAL.pdf).
- **Lai's expert summary of the key observation, verbatim [R].** "Fauzan's proof uses Hankel determinants, which were previously studied by Haynes–Zudilin [26] and Zudilin [68]. Fauzan's proof is also related to some ideas of Brown [8]. Roughly speaking, Hankel determinants yield finer p-adic estimates." and "In the literature, there are many known linear combinations of 1 and ζ(5) with rational coefficients, of the form a_{i,j} + b_{i,j}ζ(5). These linear combinations do not suffice to prove the irrationality of ζ(5). Fauzan arranged a suitable sequence of such linear forms into a Hankel determinant … assuming that ζ(5) ∈ Q, Fauzan was able to show that v_p(Ĩ_n) is much larger than the entrywise lower bound n·min v_p(a_{i,j} + b_{i,j}ζ(5)). This is the key reason why Fauzan's proof [15] works." ([26] = Haynes–Zudilin, *Hankel determinants of zeta values*, SIGMA 11 (2015), 101; [68] = Zudilin, *A determinantal approach to irrationality*, Constr. Approx. 45 (2017), 301–310; [8] = Brown, *Mellin transforms, transfinite diameter and rational approximations of integrals*, Exp. Math. (2026), doi:10.1080/10586458.2026.2695605) — [Lai, arXiv:2610.06838v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06838) ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.06838v2)).
- **FLSZ on novelty and antecedents, verbatim [R].** "On September 17, 2026, a few weeks after powerful AI tools became available, Fauzan posted online a proof that ζ(5) is irrational [7]. The principal idea was a new use of Hankel determinants." And, in their "Declaration from natural intelligence": "all the ingredients for the methodology in use here have been created before advancements of AI tools. For example, the potentials of Hankel determinant constructions are discussed in [19] and of their multivariate generalisations in [4]; the deep connection and a different perspective of the methodology are offered in the framework of holonomicity criteria in [5]." ([19] = Zudilin 2017, [4] = Brown 2026, [5] = Calegari–Dimitrov–Tang, arXiv:2408.15403) — [Fischler–Lai–Sprang–Zudilin, arXiv:2611.16156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16156) ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16156)).
- **FLSZ: where the arithmetic gain sits [R].** "While using Hankel determinants in [7] gives one access to better arithmetic than by traditional techniques, the improvement in Theorem 1.1 over the previous bound (1.3) comes entirely from the analytic estimates of the determinants." — [arXiv:2611.16156](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16156).
- **Pre-2026 Hankel-determinant literature exists [R/S].** Haynes–Zudilin, "Hankel determinants of zeta values" (arXiv:1510.01901) studies asymptotics of Hankel determinants built from ζ(an+b) in the context of irrationality (title confirmed in search results and in Lai's bibliography; abstract not opened) — [arXiv:1510.01901](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.01901).
- **The same method reportedly handles ζ(2), ζ(3), ζ(4) [R for the report, S for the source].** Qian Tang's repository README states (paraphrase) that on 24 September 2026 the blog Persiflage noted that changing Fauzan's parameters lets the same method handle ζ(k) for k = 2, 3, 4, 5 — [dtq1997/zeta2-zeta3-linear-independence](https://github.com/dtq1997/zeta2-zeta3-linear-independence); the post itself, [Persiflage, "zeta(5) is irrational", 24 Sep 2026](https://galoisrepresentations.org/2026/09/24/zeta5-is-irrational/), could not be retrieved.

### Inferences
- From (7.2): A_200 + U < −139/8000 ≈ −0.0174 with U ≈ −1.3670, so the arithmetic cost A_200 is at most ≈ 1.3496 and the proof is won on the K² scale by a relative margin of roughly 1.3%. This thinness explains why formalizers report several very tight numerical inequalities (§4).
- Relation to earlier approaches, in one line each: Apéry/Beukers — one linear form per n in 1, ζ(3), decay λⁿ against denominators d_n³; Ball–Rivoal/Zudilin — well-poised hypergeometric linear forms in several odd zeta values (hence type (ii) results); Brown–Zudilin — cellular integrals giving approximations to ζ(5) that are not good enough (exponent 0.86); Fauzan — a determinant of many individually insufficient forms in 1, ζ(5), whose p-adic valuation as a whole is far better than entrywise and whose archimedean size is Gram-determinant small (e^{−cK²}). This is a genuinely different construction from the Ball–Rivoal line, though built from known Hankel/determinantal ideas (Zudilin 2017, Haynes–Zudilin 2015, Brown 2026).
- The preprint's own bibliography omits the Hankel-determinant antecedents that Lai and FLSZ point to; the attribution of antecedents therefore comes from the experts, not from the author.

### Gaps
- The exact relation to Calegari–Dimitrov–Tang's arithmetic holonomicity bounds is asserted by FLSZ only as "a different perspective"; no source retrieved works it out.
- The Persiflage post (which reportedly gives a uniform treatment of ζ(2),…,ζ(5) and cites Krattenthaler and Zudilin for the irrationality criterion [S]) could not be read, so its mathematical content and comments are not documented here.
- Sections 4–6 and Appendices A–C of the preprint were not checked line by line in this session.

## 4. Has the proof been formally verified? What exactly do the Lean repositories prove, under which axioms or unproved inputs? Is there a journal submission or acceptance?

### Takeaway
Three public Lean 4/Mathlib developments (Romik; Firsching = mo271; Del Solar = domino14) each state that they prove `ζ(5)` irrational with no `sorry` in the proof of the final theorem: Romik's takes the prime number theorem (θ(x) ~ x) as its one extra axiom, while the other two import/port PNT from the PrimeNumberTheoremAnd project and report only Lean's three standard axioms. All three formalize Theorem 1.1 only (not Corollary 1.2), and all had to repair or alter steps of the printed argument; none was rebuilt in this session. No journal submission or acceptance was found.

### Cited Findings

## 5. Are there follow-up papers that confirm, extend, simplify or correct it? What do they say about its correctness?

### Takeaway
Two arXiv papers by established specialists build directly on the method — Lai (linear independence of 1, ζ(3), ζ(4)) and Fischler–Lai–Sprang–Zudilin (many more irrational odd zeta values) — and Lai lists five further quick follow-ups (p-adic zeta values, dilogarithms, 1, ζ(2), ζ(3), Catalan's constant), mostly Lean-formalized and mostly not on arXiv. None of them claims to correct Fauzan's theorem; both arXiv papers treat it as proved, and both declare that their *own* initial proofs were produced by GPT-6 Astra and then verified and rewritten by the authors.

### Cited Findings
- **Fischler, Lai, Sprang, Zudilin, "Many more odd zeta values are irrational", arXiv:2611.16156 [math.NT], v1 12 Nov 2026 (01:32 UTC), 17 pages, MSC 11J72; 11M06, 33C20; submitter Li Lai; preprint, no journal reference [R].** Main result (type (ii), asymptotic): "Theorem 1.1. For any ε > 0 and all sufficiently large odd integers s ≥ s₀(ε), we have N_s ≥ (√(4ζ(2)ζ(3)/ζ(6)) − ε)·√(s log log s / log s)", where N_s counts irrational values among ζ(3), ζ(5), …, ζ(s) and √(4ζ(2)ζ(3)/ζ(6)) = 2.788258…; this improves the previous N_s ≥ 1.19√(s/log s) of Lai–Yu (constant 1.28 by Lai) by a factor √(log log s) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16156); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16156).
- **FLSZ method and relation to Fauzan [R].** They replace the single polynomial P_n of Lai–Yu by a Hankel determinant Δ_{n,m} = det(L_n(t^{i+j})) of a linear functional; denominators are bounded "by considering a basis consisting of Pochhammer symbols and using standard arithmetic properties. No sophisticated p-adic estimates are in use"; "The principal novelties here lie in the non-vanishing part" (§5, Hankel matrices associated with signed measures on discrete closed subsets of the reals); "our design of the linear functional L_n deviates significantly from its prototype from [7]" — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16156).

### Inferences
- **Classification of the follow-ups.** FLSZ (asymptotic count; type (ii)) and Lai (1, ζ(3), ζ(4)) are class **(B)**: unrefereed preprints of 12 Nov and 5/15 Oct 2026 by recognised experts. ## 7. What is the strongest unconditional, accepted result about ζ(5) alone and about small sets containing ζ(5)?

### Takeaway
In the refereed literature (state before 17 Sep 2026) nothing was known about ζ(5) alone beyond non-decisive rational approximations (Brown–Zudilin, exponent 0.86); the best small-set results were Zudilin's "at least one of ζ(5), ζ(7), ζ(9), ζ(11) is irrational" (2001) and Lai–Zhou's "at least two of ζ(5), ζ(7), …, ζ(35) are irrational" (2021). As of 15 Nov 2026 the statement "ζ(5) is irrational" itself rests on Fauzan's unrefereed preprint plus three Lean formalizations and expert uptake (class A with caveats, §6); if accepted it supersedes all "at least one of …" statements containing ζ(5).

### Inferences
- **What can be stated today (15 Nov 2026).** (a) *Established in the refereed literature:* ζ(3) ∉ ℚ (Apéry); at least one of ζ(5), ζ(7), ζ(9), ζ(11) is irrational (Zudilin 2001); at least two of ζ(5), …, ζ(35) are irrational (Lai–Zhou; refereed per arXiv comment, journal reference unverified here); infinitely many odd zeta values are irrational with the quantitative bounds above. (b) *Established by formal verification and treated as proved by the specialists, but not yet refereed:* ζ(5) ∉ ℚ (Fauzan 2026). (c) *Claimed, still being evaluated:* irrationality exponent of ζ(5) ≤ 260 (Fauzan, Cor. 1.2); N_s ≥ (2.78…−ε)√(s log log s/log s) (FLSZ); linear independence of 1, ζ(3), ζ(4) (Lai). (d) *Still open as far as these sources show:* irrationality of ζ(7), ζ(9), … individually; any ℚ-linear independence statement involving ζ(5) with another zeta value (e.g. 1, ζ(3), ζ(5)); transcendence of any odd zeta value.
- If ζ(5) ∉ ℚ is accepted, "at least one of ζ(5), ζ(7), ζ(9), ζ(11)" becomes trivially true, and the natural next small-set questions become "at least one of ζ(7), ζ(9), …" and "at least two of ζ(5), …, ζ(s)" for small s; the Lai–Zhou theorem gives two irrationals among ζ(5), …, ζ(35) but does not say whether one of them is ζ(5).
- A careful one-sentence summary for a reader: *"As of 15 November 2026, the irrationality of ζ(5) has been announced in an unrefereed preprint by Aabir Fauzan (Zenodo, 17 September 2026); the statement has been machine-checked in Lean 4 by three independent projects and is being used as a theorem by Zudilin, Fischler, Sprang and Lai, but the paper itself has not been revised or published in a journal, its printed argument contains at least two gaps that the formalizations had to repair, and its quantitative corollary (irrationality exponent ≤ 260) has not been independently verified."*
