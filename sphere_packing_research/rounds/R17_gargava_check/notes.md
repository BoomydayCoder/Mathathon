# R17 -- Check of Gargava, "Lattice packings through division algebras" (arXiv:2107.04844), Theorem 16, for $D_\mathbb R=\mathbb H\times\mathbb H$

Tags: **[P]** proved here, **[L]** cited (with a note whether it was read in this session), **[GAP]** open.

**Verdict: REPAIRED.** The printed proof contains exactly one false sentence when $D_\mathbb R=\mathbb K^s$, $s\ge2$ (transitivity on $D_\mathbb R^k\setminus\{0\}$, in the proof of Lemma 19). ## 1. Exact statements [L, read]

- **Norm (App. A.1, l. 1478).** For a finite-dimensional $\mathbb R$-algebra $A$: $\mathrm{tr}_A$, $\mathrm N_A$ are the trace and determinant of the left-multiplication map. So for $a\in M_k(D_\mathbb R)$: $\mathrm N(a)=\det(L_a\mid M_k(D_\mathbb R))=\det_\mathbb R(a\mid D_\mathbb R^k)^k$ (a commented-out remark in the TeX says the same).
- **Groups (§1.2).** "$G=\{a\in M_k(D_\mathbb R)\mid \mathrm N(a)=1\}$, $\Gamma=\{a\in M_k(\mathcal O)\mid \mathrm N(a)=1\}$"; Rem. 7: "also written $SL_k(D_\mathbb R)$, $SL_k(\mathcal O)$". **This is the TOTAL norm, one real equation**, not reduced norm one at every real place.
  For $D_\mathbb R=\mathbb H\times\mathbb H$: $\det_\mathbb R(g_v\mid\mathbb H^k)=\mathrm{Nrd}(g_v)^2>0$, so
  $$G=\{(g_1,g_2)\in GL_k(\mathbb H)^2:\ \mathrm{Nrd}(g_1)\,\mathrm{Nrd}(g_2)=1\}=G^{(1)}\times Z_+,$$
  $G^{(1)}=SL_k(\mathbb H)^2$, $Z_+=\{(t,t^{-1}):t>0\}$ (central scalars); $\dim G=8k^2-1$, one more than $G^{(1)}$. $\Gamma=GL_k(\mathcal O)$ (all units of $M_k(\mathcal O)$ have $\det_\mathbb R=1$ here); $\Gamma$ is the full stabiliser of $\mathcal O^k$ in $G$ (source, proof of Lemma 19; [P]: $g\mathcal O^k=\mathcal O^k\Rightarrow ge_i\in\mathcal O^k$).
- **Prop. 14.** "The space $G/\Gamma$ carries a unique probability measure that is left-invariant over the action of $G$." Proof: Siegel set $\mathfrak S^*$ with $\mathfrak S^*\Gamma=G$ (Lemma 12, from Thm 4 = [W58, Thm 2] and Lemma 6 = [W58, Lemma 10.2]) has finite Haar measure (Cor. 10). Rem. 15: also from Borel-Harish-Chandra.
- **Thm 16.** "Let $D$ be a $\mathbb Q$-division algebra containing an order $\mathcal O\subseteq D$. Let $G=SL_k(D_\mathbb R)$ and $\Gamma=SL_k(\mathcal O)$, for some $k\ge2$. Let $dg$ be the probability measure on $G/\Gamma$ that is left-invariant under $G$ action. Then for any $f\in C_c(D_\mathbb R^k)$, $\int_{G/\Gamma}\big(\sum_{v\in g\mathcal O^k\setminus\{0\}}f(v)\big)dg=\int_{D_\mathbb R^k}f(x)\,dx$, where $dx$ is a Lebesgue measure on $D_\mathbb R^k$ with respect to which $\mathcal O^k$ has a covolume of $1$."
- **Rem. 17.** "Through a small application of the dominated convergence theorem, one can take $f$ to be any Riemann integrable compactly supported function."
- **Lemma 18.** For $f\in C_c$, $\Phi_f(g\Gamma)=\sum_{v\in g\mathcal O^k\setminus0}f(v)$ is absolutely integrable, and $\varepsilon^{dk}\Phi_{f_\varepsilon}$, $0<\varepsilon\le1$, $f_\varepsilon(x)=f(\varepsilon x)$, $d=\dim_\mathbb R D_\mathbb R$, is dominated uniformly in $\varepsilon$ by an integrable function.
- **Lemma 19.** $\int\Phi_f=\int\varepsilon^{dk}\Phi_{f_\varepsilon}$ for all $\varepsilon>0$ (proved in the form $\int\Phi_f=C\int f\,dx$).

## 2. The printed proof, and where $s\ge2$ enters

Structure: Thm 16 $\Leftarrow$ Lemma 18 + Lemma 19 + Riemann sums.

1. **Constant (proof of Thm 16).** $\int\Phi_f=\int\varepsilon^{dk}\Phi_{f_\varepsilon}\to\int f\,dx$ by dominated convergence, since pointwise $\varepsilon^{dk}\Phi_{f_\varepsilon}(g\Gamma)\to\int f\,dx$ ($\mathrm{covol}(g\mathcal O^k)=1$). Uses: $|\det_\mathbb R g|=1$ on $G$ (true for total norm one), $dg$ a probability measure, Lemma 18. **No transitivity used; $s$ irrelevant.** [checked]
2. **Lemma 18 (integrability).** $\int_{G/\Gamma}\le\int_{\mathfrak S^*}$; $\mathfrak S^*=\bigcup_i\mathrm N(b_i)^{1/dk}(KA^{(1)}_{\omega_1}A^\mathbb R_{c_1}N_{c_2})b_i^{-1}$; $K$-invariance of balls; $b_i^{-1}\mathcal O^k\subseteq\frac1N\mathcal O^k$; $a'an=(a'ana'^{-1})a'$ with $\{a'ana'^{-1}\}$ relatively compact; point count $\le\prod_i(B_1+B_2(R'/(a'_{ii}\varepsilon))^d)$; convergence of $\int_{A^\mathbb R_{c_1}}\prod_i(B_1+B_2(R'/a'_{ii})^d)\prod_{i<j}(a'_{ii}/a'_{jj})^d\,da'$. I re-did the exponent count: with $y_j=a'_{jj}/a'_{j+1,j+1}\in(0,c_1]$ and $I\subseteq\{1,\dots,k\}$ the set of factors where $B_2(\cdot)$ is chosen, the exponent of $y_j$ against $dy_j/y_j$ is $d\,(\tfrac jk\#I-\#I_{\le j}+j(k-j))>0$ (as $\#I_{\le j}\le j\le j(k-j)$, with equality only for $j=k-1$, $\#I_{\le j}=k-1$, and then $\#I>0$). [P] **No transitivity used.** Where $s\ge2$ matters: $D_\mathbb R^{(1)}=\{\mathrm N=1\}$ and hence $A^{(1)}$ are non-compact (for $\mathbb H\times\mathbb H$ they contain $(t,t^{-1})$); the proof only integrates over the relatively compact piece $A^{(1)}_{\omega_1}$, and that this suffices is exactly condition (a) of Thm 4 ($d_{ii}/\mathrm{tr}(d_{ii})\in\omega_0$, $\omega_0$ relatively compact in $D_\mathbb R^\times$, Rem. 5), i.e. the unit-group compactness built into [W58, Thm 2]. For $s=1$ condition (a) is vacuous; for $s\ge2$ it carries real content and is **cited, not proved**, in the source.
3. **Prop. 14 / Lemma 12 / Cor. 10 / Prop. 51.** Independent of transitivity. Minor slips, harmless for $\mathbb H\times\mathbb H$: (i) in Prop. 51 the cone $(0,1]U$ has infinite Haar measure in $GL_k(D_\mathbb R)$ (use $[1,e]U$; or: $G=\ker\mathrm N$ is a closed normal subgroup of a unimodular group, hence unimodular); (ii) signs of $\mathrm N$ of units/unitaries in Lemma 6/12 (only relevant with real places and odd $k$; for $\mathbb H\times\mathbb H$ all real determinants are positive); (iii) in Lemma 12 the display proves the converse of the needed implication for $c_1$, the needed one follows from the same computation; $C,C'$ are swapped once.
4. **Lemma 19 (unfolding) -- THE PROBLEMATIC STEP.** TeX l. 993 (PDF: proof of Lemma 19, p. 13): "there is a homeomorphism $G/G_v\simeq D_\mathbb R^k\setminus\{0\}$ given by $gG_v\mapsto gv$. Note that, this works out because $G$ acts transitively on $D^k_\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$", and l. 1002: "since $G/G_v\simeq V\setminus\{0\}$, the integral $\int_{G/G_v}f(h_1v)dh_1=c_v\int_{D_\mathbb R^k\setminus\{0\}}f(x)dx$". **False for $s\ge2$**: $G\subseteq\prod_vGL_k(\mathbb K_v)$ preserves each stratum $\{x:x^{(v)}=0\text{ exactly for }v\in S\}$; for $\mathbb H\times\mathbb H$ the orbits are $\{0\}$, $(\mathbb H^k\setminus0)\times0$, $0\times(\mathbb H^k\setminus0)$ and $\Omega$. The rest of Lemma 19 (decomposition into $\Gamma$-orbits, Tonelli, unfolding $G/\Gamma\to G/\Gamma_v\to G/G_v$, unimodularity of $G_v$, finiteness of $\mathrm{vol}(G_v/\Gamma_v)$) only uses the orbit of a fixed $v\in\mathcal O^k\setminus0$.

So the proof as printed is literally correct only for $s=1$ ($D_\mathbb R$ a division algebra); for $s\ge2$ it needs the local repair of §3. The group is neither a product of $SL_k(\mathbb K_v)$ (it is bigger by the $(s-1)$-dimensional central factor $Z_+$) nor $GL$; this matters for finite covolume ($Z_+/(\text{units})$ compact by Dirichlet; inside [W58, Thm 2] resp. BHC) but not for the orbit argument.

## 3. Repair [P]

Setting: $D_\mathbb R\cong\prod_{v=1}^s\mathbb K_v$ with $\mathbb K_v\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C,\mathbb H\}$ (e.g. $\mathbb H\times\mathbb H$), $k\ge2$, $V=D_\mathbb R^k=\prod_v\mathbb K_v^k$, $n=dk$, $G,\Gamma,dg$ as in §1, $\Omega:=\prod_v(\mathbb K_v^k\setminus\{0\})$ (open, dense, complement = finite union of proper subspaces, Lebesgue-null).

**(R1) [P] Lattice vectors lie in $\Omega$.** If $w=(w_1,\dots,w_k)\in D^k\setminus\{0\}$, some $w_i\ne0$ is invertible in $D$, hence in $D_\mathbb R$, hence all its components $w_i^{(v)}\ne0$; so $w\in\Omega$. As $G\subseteq\prod_vGL_k(\mathbb K_v)$ preserves $\Omega$: $g\mathcal O^k\setminus\{0\}\subseteq\Omega$ for all $g\in G$.

**(R2) [P] $G$ is transitive on $\Omega$ (needs $k\ge2$).** Let $x,y\in\Omega$. Choose $h_v\in GL_k(\mathbb K_v)$ with $h_vx^{(v)}=y^{(v)}$ (a nonzero vector over a division ring is the first column of an invertible matrix). The stabiliser of $y^{(v)}$ in $GL_k(\mathbb K_v)$ is conjugate to that of $e_1$, which contains $\mathrm{diag}(1,c,1,\dots,1)$, $c\in\mathbb K_v^\times$, of real determinant $N_{\mathbb K_v/\mathbb R}(c)$: every positive real, and every nonzero real if $\mathbb K_v=\mathbb R$. Multiplying the $h_v$ on the left by such elements we get $\det_\mathbb R(h_v)>0$ for all $v$ and then $\prod_v\det_\mathbb R(h_v)=1$; then $g=(h_v)_v$ has $\mathrm N(g)=1$ and $gx=y$. (The same proof shows that $G^{(1)}=\prod_vSL_k(\mathbb K_v)$ is transitive on $\Omega$. For $k=1$ it fails.) Hence $Gw=\Omega$ for every $w\in\mathcal O^k\setminus0$, and $G/G_w\to\Omega$ is a diffeomorphism (transitive smooth action of a second countable Lie group; [L] standard, Helgason DGLGSS II.3.2/II.4.3).

**(R3) Minimal repair of the printed proof.** In Lemma 19 replace $D_\mathbb R^k\setminus\{0\}$ by $\Omega$: by (R1),(R2) $G/G_v\simeq\Omega$; Lebesgue measure on $\Omega$ is $G$-invariant ($|\det_\mathbb Rg|=1$), so $G_v$ is unimodular and $\int_{G/G_v}f(hv)\,dh=c_v\int_\Omega f\,dx=c_v\int_Vf\,dx$. All other lines are unchanged. [P]

**(R4) Self-contained version of the orbit step (avoids unfolding).** For Borel $B\subseteq V$ put $\nu(B):=\int_{G/\Gamma}\#\big((g\mathcal O^k\setminus0)\cap B\big)\,dg\in[0,\infty]$.
- (a) [P] $\nu$ is a Borel measure (monotone convergence; $g\mapsto\sum_{w\in\mathcal O^k\setminus0}\mathbf 1_B(gw)$ is Borel and right $\Gamma$-invariant) and $\nu(hB)=\nu(B)$ for $h\in G$ (invariance of $dg$).
- (b) [P] $\nu(V\setminus\Omega)=0$ by (R1).
- (c) [L, source Lemma 18, proof read and re-checked in §2.2; depends on [W58, Thm 2], [W58, Lemma 10.2] not read] $\nu$ is finite on compact sets, and for $0\le f\in C_c(V)$ the function $H_f:=\sup_{0<\varepsilon\le1}\varepsilon^{n}\Phi_{f_\varepsilon}$ is integrable on $G/\Gamma$. (Detail [P]: $H_f$ is measurable, as the sup may be taken over rational $\varepsilon$; the source bounds it on $\mathfrak S^*$ by $M\max_i\prod_l(B_1+B_2(R'/a'_{ll})^d)$, whose integral over $\mathfrak S^*$, which contains a Borel fundamental domain, is finite.)
- (d) [P + L standard] By (c) $\nu|_\Omega$ and $\mathrm{Leb}|_\Omega$ are $G$-invariant Radon measures on $\Omega\cong G/G_w$. An invariant Radon measure on a homogeneous space $G/H$ is unique up to a scalar ([L] Weil; Bourbaki Int. VII §2 Th. 3; Folland, Abstract Harmonic Analysis, Thm 2.49; no hypothesis on $H$ beyond closedness is needed for uniqueness). So $\nu|_\Omega=C\,\mathrm{Leb}|_\Omega$, $0\le C<\infty$, and by (b) and $\mathrm{Leb}(V\setminus\Omega)=0$: $\nu=C\,\mathrm{Leb}$ on $V$. Thus $\int_{G/\Gamma}\Phi_f\,dg=C\int_Vf\,dx$ for $0\le f\in C_c(V)$.
- (e) [P, the source's argument] $C=1$: apply (d) to $f_\varepsilon$: $\int\varepsilon^n\Phi_{f_\varepsilon}dg=C\int f\,dx$ for all $\varepsilon$. Pointwise $\varepsilon^n\Phi_{f_\varepsilon}(g\Gamma)=\varepsilon^n\sum_{u\in g\mathcal O^k\setminus0}f(\varepsilon u)\to\int f\,dx$ (Riemann sums, $\mathrm{covol}(g\mathcal O^k)=|\det_\mathbb Rg|=1$). Dominated convergence with $H_f$ and $\int dg=1$ give $\int f\,dx=C\int f\,dx$. (Fatou alone gives $C\ge1$ without the uniform domination.)
- General $f\in C_c$: split into positive and negative parts. $\square$

**(R5) [P] Variant for reduced norm one at each place.** Let $G^{(1)}=\prod_vSL_k(\mathbb K_v)$, $\Gamma^{(1)}=\Gamma\cap G^{(1)}$, case $s=2$, $D_\mathbb R=\mathbb H\times\mathbb H$. $G=Z_+\times G^{(1)}$, $p:G\to Z_+$; $\Lambda=p(\Gamma)$ is discrete (reduced norms of units are units of the centre). If $\mathcal F_Z$, $\mathcal F_1$ are fundamental domains for $Z_+/\Lambda$ and $G^{(1)}/\Gamma^{(1)}$, then $\mathcal F_Z\times\mathcal F_1$ is one for $G/\Gamma$; hence $\mathrm{vol}(G^{(1)}/\Gamma^{(1)})<\infty$ and the $G$-mean is the average over $z\in Z_+/\Lambda$ of the $G^{(1)}$-means $\nu_z(B)=\int_{G^{(1)}/\Gamma^{(1)}}\#(zg\mathcal O^k\setminus0\cap B)dg=\nu_1(z^{-1}B)$. Each $\nu_z$ is $G^{(1)}$-invariant, carried by $\Omega$, so $\nu_1=C_1\mathrm{Leb}$ with $C_1\in[0,\infty]$ (if $\nu_1$ is not locally finite then, by transitivity and invariance, it is infinite on every open set; set $C_1=\infty$), and $\nu_z=C_1\mathrm{Leb}$ because $z$ preserves Lebesgue measure. Averaging: $C_1=1$. So the mean value formula also holds over $G^{(1)}/\Gamma^{(1)}=SL_k(\mathbb H)^2/SL_k(\mathcal O)$. (Sketch level; not needed for the project.)

## 4. Conclusion

- Theorem 16 may be relied upon for $D_\mathbb R=\mathbb H\times\mathbb H$ (icosians, $\mathbb H\otimes\mathbb Q(\sqrt{13})$-type algebras, any order $\mathcal O$) in every rank $k\ge2$, with the reading **$G=\{g\in GL_k(\mathbb H)^2:\mathrm{Nrd}(g_1)\mathrm{Nrd}(g_2)=1\}$ (total norm one), $\Gamma=GL_k(\mathcal O)=\mathrm{Stab}_G(\mathcal O^k)$, $dg$ the invariant probability measure, $dx$ normalised by $\mathrm{covol}(\mathcal O^k)=1$**. By (R5) the reading $SL_k(\mathbb H)^2/SL_k(\mathcal O)$ is equally valid. Every $g\in G$ preserves Lebesgue measure, so all lattices $g\mathcal O^k$ have covolume 1 (consistent with R6 Lemma 4.2).
- The caveat of R6 Remark 4.1a(iv) is resolved: the false transitivity claim is confined to one sentence of Lemma 19, and the repair is (R1)-(R3).
